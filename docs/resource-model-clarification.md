# Resource-model clarification for preprint v0.1.0

Recorded after the Claude/Agy/Grok review request, 28 October 2026. This clarification is a post-publication companion document; it is not present in the immutable v0.1.0 Zenodo archive. The numerical theorem bounds and algebraic/probabilistic hypotheses are unchanged. Complete resource certification in Lean remains unfinished.

The intended computational model for Section 6 is a bit-cost random-access model with explicit finite binary arrays. Reading, writing and Boolean operations on a bit are charged. Address and index manipulation is also charged at its bit length; addressing polynomial-sized work arrays adds logarithmic factors in n. Field operations expand to their polynomial-representative bit algorithms. Whole unbounded integers, field operations, allocation/copying, and matrix operations are not unit-cost primitives.

The read-only input and serialized output are finite binary strings. The current candidate and work arrays have polynomial size in n and are discarded or reused between trials. Fair bits arrive sequentially from a read-only source; the mathematical full-tape probability space is not allocated as working memory. Only the trial/counter representation needs O(R+log(h+1)) bits depending on h. Its arithmetic and the cost of reading parameters are charged. This interpretation does not assert the same exponent for arbitrary machine-model simulations, such as naive random-access simulation on a single-tape Turing machine.

Under this model, the elementary stage algorithms of Section 6 retain their displayed polynomial exponents up to logarithmic addressing factors. The loose soft-O(2^(4R) h n^12) time and soft-O(n^8+log(h+1)) space envelopes remain the formal targets. The soft-O allowance is an upper bound; allowing logarithmic dependence on h does not imply the implementation actually allocates a multiplicative number of trial workspaces. In particular, a logarithmic factor in h is not a factor N=8*2^(4R)h. The formalization should establish a separate live-work-array bound and an additive counter bound, then derive the published envelope. No strengthened tilde-free bound is claimed from review alone.

For the Lean constructor, the proposed implementation strategy is a cost-instrumented finite-array computation whose erasure equals the functional algorithm, with proved bit-cost implementations of its primitives and explicit current/peak live-storage accounting. A counter of abstract field operations alone, an assumed interpreter overhead, or total allocation without a peak-space bound will not certify the resource theorem. A small arithmetic/elimination prototype should validate this strategy before the full constructor is built.

Python's immutable packed-integer construction is separately discussed as an implementation accounting observation. Complete certification of the mathematical constructor will not certify Python runtime behavior without a separate source-refinement and cost-correspondence proof. The post-DOI plan now requires the exact resource model, representation choices and asymptotic convention to be frozen in a reviewed specification, with this clarification explicitly recorded as part of the certification target. Any later paper/certification release must identify the clarification rather than silently attributing it to the original archive.
